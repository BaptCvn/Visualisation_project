Visualisation 1 : With this visualisation we can precisely observe how the most popular baby names evolve from 1900 to 2020. This visualisation mainly focuses on popular baby names. It shows very clearly how some names remain popular over long period of times, while others come and go. As a side effect of this visualisation, we can observe trends in time like the Worl War 1 declining birth rate or the post Worl War 2 baby boom.
This visualisation however does not take into account unpopular baby names. It also do not represent the entirety of the data by only taing the 10 most popular names and leaving everything else out. Finally, it can sometimes be a bit tricky to observe quick changes in the popularity of names when they change year to year.

Visualisation 2 : Here, we choose to aggregate the data over the whole period to get a clear understanding of the most popular baby name of the 20th century in each region. This representation clearly demonstrate that the country is divided between to very popular names (Jean or Marie) and that other names are outliers compared to them. To further the interest of this map, clicking on a department will create a bar plot showing the 10 most popular names of the century in said department. 
This visualisation is very intuitive and convey the information simply and accurately. Its interactivity is also one of its strength.
It is a shame however that we had to aggregate the data over time to get a clear representation, as we lose all sense of time variation. We also do not get to compare gender between them, Mary and Jean being the most popular baby names overall but probably not in a single gender. Finally, it can be a bit difficult to compare departments with each other as the bar plot change once another department is selected.

Visualisation 3 : In this visualisation , we specifically picked three baby names to show some pattern in the data. Most of the names are gendered, meaning they are either popular with baby boys or baby girls (as this data set treats sex as binary, which is a simplification but does not generally hold). Some of them however, switch popularity between genders. The whole point of this visualisation is to prove that some names are inherently gender specific, masculine or feminine (at least in our given timeframe) while others are more gender neutral.
One of the problem of this visualisation however is that it cannot be visually clear if we take every name into account, hence why we had to limit ourselves to three handpicked names. We also focus heavily on this particular effect of gender in the data and possibly miss some other effects.
